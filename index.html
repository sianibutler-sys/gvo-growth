GVO Sound growth plan

Fill The Rooms. Prove The Spend. Build The System.

A plan for Cory and Siani to walk through together. Prices come from gvosound.com. Numbers marked "working number" are placeholders until GVO's real figures go in. Every calculator updates as you type, and every section ends with a space to record what we decide.

THE SHORT VERSION
  1. The target is the monthly gap between what GVO earns now and its roughly $5,000 in costs.
  2. Every ad dollar has to come back as at least $1 of take-home. Anything that doesn't gets cut.
  3. Revenue comes in three layers: memberships and retainers for a steady floor, content projects for big tickets, and sessions, photo, events and apparel for volume.
  4. Start small: about $20 a day in ads for 90 days, then scale only what proved itself.
  5. First proof: R&B & Vibes on Nov 27 and the Valentine's cruise.
  6. GVO owns every account, contact and asset built for it.

How ad spend works

Ads buy attention. What matters is how many dollars it takes to win one paying client, and whether that client is worth more than that.

You set a daily budget on Instagram, Facebook or Google. The platform shows the ad to the people you target and charges as they see or tap it. Some of those people book. A client who books once is worth $80 to GVO. A client who books three times is worth $240. Repeat business is where ads pay off.

Where the dollars go

  • Google Search reaches people already looking, like "recording studio Annapolis." High intent, smaller volume.
  • Instagram and Facebook build demand with session clips and event recaps, and retarget gvosound.com visitors who didn't book.
  • Each ad points to its own page on gvosound.com with a tracked link, so every booking traces back to the ad that earned it.
Decide today

  • Which services to test first: photo, recording, cruise, events
  • Any past ad accounts or results
  • Who approves a budget increase

Three revenue layers

A steady month needs a floor that refills itself, a few big tickets, and volume to fill the gaps.

Recurring

The floor

Memberships for artists and podcasters who record often. Monthly content packages for local businesses. Money in before the month starts.

High-ticket

The upside

Content production by quote: brand videos, product shoots, event coverage, podcast production. One project can equal 15–20 sessions.

Volume

The fill

Recording, photo rentals, events and apparel, driven by ads, Google, print and the event crowd.

Membership, working example

Deep hourly discounts don't work, because the engineer costs $35 an hour whatever the client pays. The value comes from perks that cost GVO little.

Perks: priority booking, a merch discount, early access to drops, members-only listening nights, and a feature on @gvo.sound. Fifteen members bring in about $2,100 a month before a single walk-in booking. Unused hours don't roll over, and members can't take every Friday night.

Content packages, working example

A portfolio page built from GVO's existing work, a quote form, and three clear starting points: a single shoot day, a monthly content package, and full podcast production. Sold mostly through referrals and direct outreach to local businesses, with ads in support.

Ground rules worth settling early

The things that cause problems later if nobody decides them now.

Spending

  • A monthly ad cap that never moves without Cory's OK
  • Any ad that costs more to win a client than GVO keeps from that client after two weeks gets paused
  • Ad spend is billed to GVO's card directly by Meta and Google, never routed through Siani

Ownership and access

  • Ad accounts live in GVO's name. Instagram is already in Cory's Meta business portfolio.
  • Siani gets access to run them. Cory and Ashley keep full control.
  • All contact lists, content and accounts belong to GVO.

Rights and permission

  • Written OK from artists before their tracks, faces or names run in ads
  • Photo and video releases for clients and event guests who appear in ads
  • Marketing texts and emails only to people who opted in

Reputation

  • Every client gets a review request by text after their session. Google reviews feed search results.

Kept separate

  • No Deadbeats Club stays apart from GVO revenue. If it ever becomes a client, that's its own agreement.
  • Travel is tracked but doesn't count toward the floor until the margin is known.
Decide today
  • Monthly ad cap
  • Release forms: use existing ones or create new
  • Whether No Deadbeats Club fits anywhere, and how Cory wants it handled

Shipping

Square already handles it. The decisions are how to price it and who packs.

Square supports flat rates, live carrier rates and free shipping above an order total, and labels print straight from the Square Dashboard. Square is moving shipping settings from Square Online into the main Dashboard, so they may show up in a new place.

Working recommendation

  • One flat rate on most orders, so nobody gets surprised at checkout
  • Free shipping just above the typical order total, so buyers add one more piece
  • Free local pickup at 109 Gibralter Ave
  • Labels printed from Square, so tracking reaches the customer automatically
Cory's shipping questions
  • Who packs and ships, and how fast after an order?
  • Typical package sizes: tees, hoodies, jackets, sets
  • Domestic only, or international too?
  • Average order total, to set the free-shipping line
